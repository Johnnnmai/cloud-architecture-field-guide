# Behavioral and Client-Facing Questions

These are typically "tell me about a time you ..." questions, adapted for the solutions architect context.

Based on candidate reports from Reddit, X, and personal blogs about what they were actually asked in solutions architect interviews.


## Format

Typically 30-60 minutes, conversational. The interviewer picks a theme (customer engagement, technical leadership, conflict resolution, architecture decision-making) and goes deep on one or two scenarios rather than asking many surface-level questions.

Common formats:

- Single-scenario deep dive: the interviewer picks one situation from your experience and spends most of the round exploring it from every angle
- Category-based rotation: the interviewer covers 3-4 topics (customer challenge, technical leadership, failure, stakeholder management) with one question each


## Common Behavioral Questions

### Customer Engagement and Presales

- Tell me about a time you led a technical discovery session with a customer. How did you uncover their real requirements beyond what they initially asked for?
- Describe a situation where a customer's architecture request was technically possible but not the right approach. How did you guide them to a better solution?
- Tell me about a time you built a proof of concept that directly influenced a customer's purchasing decision. What made it convincing?
- Describe a time when you had to deliver bad news to a customer - their current architecture would not meet their stated requirements. How did you handle it?
- Tell me about a customer engagement where you had to simplify a complex technical concept for a non-technical executive audience.
- Describe a time you lost a deal or a customer chose a competitor's solution. What did you learn?
- Tell me about a time you helped a customer avoid an expensive architectural mistake.


### Technical Leadership and Architecture Decisions

- Tell me about a time you had to make an architecture decision with incomplete information. What was the decision, what did you not know, and how did it turn out?
- Describe a situation where you disagreed with a senior engineer or architect on an architecture approach. How did you resolve it?
- Tell me about a time you introduced a new technology or pattern to an organization. How did you drive adoption?
- Describe an architecture decision you made that you would change if you could go back. What did you learn?
- Tell me about a time you had to set architecture standards or governance for a team or organization. How did you balance consistency with team autonomy?
- Describe a time you identified a critical technical risk in an architecture design that others had missed.
- Tell me about a time you mentored a junior engineer or architect. What was the situation and what was the outcome?


### Cross-Functional Collaboration and Stakeholder Management

- Tell me about a time you had to align engineering, security, and business stakeholders on an architecture decision with competing priorities.
- Describe a situation where you worked with a sales team to scope a technical solution. How did you balance commercial pressure with technical accuracy?
- Tell me about a time you had to push back on a timeline or scope that was technically unrealistic. How did you communicate this?
- Describe a time you worked with a security team that blocked an architecture proposal. How did you find a path forward?
- Tell me about a time you coordinated an architecture change across multiple teams or business units.
- Describe a situation where you had to translate a complex architecture into a business proposal or SOW for a customer.


### Failure and Recovery

- Tell me about a production outage caused by an architecture flaw. What was the root cause, how did you respond, and what changed?
- Describe a migration that went wrong. What happened, how did you recover, and what would you do differently?
- Tell me about a time your cost estimate for a cloud architecture was significantly off. What caused the discrepancy and how did you address it?
- Describe a situation where you designed an architecture that did not meet its performance or reliability targets in production. What did you learn?
- Tell me about a time you had to roll back an architecture change in production. What was the impact and what processes changed afterward?


### Amazon Leadership Principles (for AWS SA roles)

AWS SA interviews heavily test Leadership Principles. Common ones:

- Customer Obsession: Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer even when it was not in the original scope.
- Ownership: Tell me about a time you identified a problem outside your direct responsibility and took action.
- Dive Deep: Describe a situation where you had to investigate a complex technical issue by going several layers deeper than the surface symptoms.
- Bias for Action: Tell me about a time you made a significant architecture decision quickly because waiting would have been more costly than being wrong.
- Earn Trust: Tell me about a time you had to rebuild trust with a customer or stakeholder after a technical failure.
- Think Big: Describe a time you proposed an architecture that was significantly larger in scope than what was requested, and it turned out to be the right call.
- Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit: Tell me about a time you disagreed with a customer's or leader's technical direction. How did you handle it, and what was the outcome?
- Deliver Results: Tell me about a time you delivered a complex architecture project under tight constraints.


### Microsoft Competencies (for Azure SA roles)

Microsoft interviews often test these competencies:

- Growth Mindset: Describe a time you had to learn a new technology or domain quickly for a customer engagement.
- Customer Focus: Tell me about a time you turned around a struggling customer relationship through technical guidance.
- Collaboration: Describe a situation where you worked across teams (engineering, sales, support) to deliver a customer outcome.
- Drive for Results: Tell me about a complex, multi-phase project you drove to completion.
